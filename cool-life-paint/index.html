Did You Know?

The science behind reflective exterior paint — and why it matters more in the Coachella Valley than almost anywhere else.

~50°F

The typical surface temperature difference between a dark stucco wall and a white reflective-coated wall on the same hot desert afternoon — that's heat that never enters your home.

Zone 15

Palm Springs sits in California Climate Zone 15 — one of the hottest zones in the US building code system — where reflective exterior surfaces deliver maximum cooling benefit.

0.80+

Solar reflectance rating of quality cool-pigment exterior paint — compared to under 0.20 for a standard dark exterior. The higher the number, the more heat bounces off instead of absorbing.

All walls

Cool Life paint is applied to all exterior wall surfaces — south, west, east, and north — plus floors. Full coverage means your entire building envelope works to keep heat out.

How Cool Life Paint Keeps Your Home Cooler

In the desert, your exterior walls are bombarded by direct sun all day. Standard paint absorbs that solar radiation and transfers the heat through the wall into your living space — forcing your A/C to compensate. Cool Life paint intercepts that heat before it enters.

  • →Less heat through the walls = less work for your A/C. Reflective exterior paint on stucco and masonry walls reduces the heat load entering through the building envelope, directly cutting how hard your cooling system has to work.
  • →Elastomeric formula seals hairline cracks. Desert stucco develops hairline cracks from thermal cycling. Our elastomeric paint flexes with the surface, bridging cracks and providing a seamless, durable finish that regular paint cannot.
  • →Extends exterior paint life. Reflective surfaces run cooler, which means less UV degradation and thermal stress on the paint film itself — your exterior paint lasts longer between repaints.
  • →Floor and patio surfaces included. Concrete floors absorb and radiate heat that makes garages, covered patios, and pool decks uncomfortably hot. Reflective floor coatings cut surface temps and protect the slab.
  • →Works with what you have. Cool Life paint goes over existing stucco, concrete block, and masonry without requiring replacement or major surface work — just proper prep and the right product.

The paint technology in plain terms

Solar reflectance

Measures how much sunlight bounces off the wall surface. A white reflective exterior paint scores 0.80+ — meaning 80% of solar energy bounces off. Standard dark paint is often below 0.10. The difference in absorbed heat is enormous.

Cool-pigment technology

Modern cool paints use specially engineered pigments that reflect infrared radiation (heat) even in mid-tone colors. A medium tan with cool pigments can outperform a dark standard paint by a significant margin in thermal performance.

Elastomeric vs. standard paint

Standard paint is thin and brittle in desert heat. Elastomeric exterior paint is significantly thicker, flexible, and crack-bridging — it handles the expansion and contraction of stucco and masonry through desert temperature swings without peeling.

Common Questions About Cool Life Paint

Everything you should know before committing.

How is Cool Life Paint different from regular exterior paint?

Regular exterior paint is primarily decorative. Cool Life uses elastomeric paint with reflective-pigment technology — it is thicker, more flexible, and engineered to reflect solar infrared radiation rather than just covering a surface. The result is a wall that stays significantly cooler in direct sun, reducing the heat transferred into your home. It also bridges hairline cracks in stucco that standard paint cannot seal.

What surfaces can Cool Life Paint be applied to?

We apply Cool Life paint to stucco (the most common exterior in the desert), concrete block and CMU masonry, painted brick, and concrete floors including garage slabs, covered patios, and pool decks. We assess the surface condition and substrate type before recommending a product — not all surfaces take the same primer or formulation.

Do I have to use white? Can I choose a color?

White delivers the highest solar reflectance, but you are not limited to white. Modern cool-pigment technology allows light to mid-tone colors — sand, warm gray, light tan, and similar desert tones — that still significantly outperform standard paint in heat rejection. We bring physical color samples so you can see the finish against your existing exterior before committing.

Can Cool Life Paint go over existing exterior paint?

In most cases, yes — as long as the existing paint is well-bonded and not peeling, chalking heavily, or bubbling. We power wash the surface, check adhesion, and apply a bonding primer where needed before the topcoat. If the existing paint is failing, it needs to come off first. Painting over a poorly-adhered base is the most common reason exterior paint fails early — we don't skip the assessment.

How long does a Cool Life exterior paint job last?

A properly prepped and applied elastomeric exterior paint job in the desert typically lasts 10–15 years before a full recoat is needed. Desert UV and heat are hard on paint, which is why the reflective formulation matters — it runs cooler, ages more slowly, and maintains its finish longer than standard paint under the same conditions. Annual visual inspection is all the maintenance typically needed.

How long does the job take?

Most single-family exterior paint projects take one to three days depending on home size, surface condition, and number of coats required. We power wash and prep on day one, prime where needed, and apply finish coats with proper dry time between. Desert heat actually speeds dry time significantly — most of the schedule is driven by surface prep, not waiting for paint to dry.

What results should I realistically expect from Cool Life Paint?

A reflective coating reduces how much sun the painted surface absorbs, so walls and roofs stay cooler to the touch and less heat soaks into the house. How much you notice indoors depends on sun exposure, wall and roof color, insulation, windows, and your AC system. Results also depend on surface preparation: cracks, failing old paint, and dirty stucco need to be addressed first. We look at all of this during the free on-site estimate and tell you honestly where it will and will not make a difference.

What affects the cost of Cool Life Paint?

The cost depends on your home: the size and type of surface (stucco walls, block walls, or a roof), the repairs and prep needed, the number of coats, and access.
