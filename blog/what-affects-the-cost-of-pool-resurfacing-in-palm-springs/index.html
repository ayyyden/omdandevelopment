What Affects the Cost of Pool Resurfacing in Palm Springs

From material choices to hidden repairs, here's what Palm Springs homeowners should know about the factors that shape pool resurfacing costs.

By Omdan Development Inc. — Licensed Contractor, CSLB #1148568

Why Pool Resurfacing Costs Vary So Much

If you've started researching pool resurfacing, you've probably noticed that there's no single, universal answer when it comes to cost. That's because every pool is unique — different in size, shape, age, and condition. A small plunge pool in Palm Desert and a sprawling freeform pool in Rancho Mirage are going to require very different amounts of labor and materials.

Beyond the pool itself, local factors like desert climate, water chemistry history, and the accessibility of your backyard all play a role. Understanding what drives these costs helps you make better decisions, set realistic expectations, and avoid surprises once the project begins. Below, we break down the most important factors that affect the total investment in your pool resurfacing project.

Pool Size and Shape

This one is straightforward: the larger your pool's surface area, the more material and labor it takes to resurface it. A compact rectangular pool requires less work than a large kidney-shaped pool with multiple curves, steps, a built-in spa, and various depth changes. Every additional square foot of surface increases the amount of finish material needed.

Shape complexity matters too. Pools with tight curves, raised walls, bench seats, sun shelves, or Baja steps require more detailed hand-application work. These features mean the crew spends more time on careful finishing, which influences the overall scope and timeline of the project.

When we visit your home for an on-site estimate, we measure the exact surface area and note every feature so the quote reflects your pool — not a generic average.

Surface Material Selection

The finish material you choose is one of the single biggest factors in the cost of resurfacing. There are several categories homeowners in Palm Springs, Cathedral City, and the broader Coachella Valley typically consider:

  • Standard white plaster: The most traditional and budget-friendly option. It provides a clean, classic look but tends to have a shorter lifespan in harsh desert conditions compared to premium finishes.
  • Quartz aggregate: A step up from basic plaster, quartz finishes blend colored quartz granules into the plaster for improved durability and a more refined appearance. They resist staining and etching better than plain plaster.
  • Pebble finishes (PebbleTec, PebbleSheen, etc.): These premium finishes use small natural pebbles for a textured surface that's exceptionally durable and long-lasting. They come in a wide variety of colors and blends and hold up particularly well in the intense Coachella Valley sun.
  • Glass bead finishes: Recycled glass beads create a smooth, shimmering surface. These are a higher-end option that adds a striking visual element, especially with underwater lighting.

Each material has a different cost per application, different preparation requirements, and a different expected lifespan. A knowledgeable contractor can help you weigh aesthetics, durability, and budget to find the right fit for your home and how you use your pool.

Structural Repairs and Prep Work

What's lurking beneath the old surface can significantly affect the scope — and cost — of a resurfacing project. When the existing finish is removed, it's not uncommon to find cracks, delamination, hollow spots, or even plumbing issues that need to be addressed before any new material goes on.

In desert communities like Indio, Desert Hot Springs, and Yucca Valley, years of extreme heat, UV exposure, and mineral-heavy fill water can accelerate surface deterioration. Pools that have been neglected or left unfilled can develop structural cracks from ground shifting and thermal expansion. These repairs — whether it's patching cracks, applying a bond coat, or fixing corroded return fittings — add to the project but are absolutely essential for a resurfacing job that lasts.

Skipping structural prep to save on the front end almost always leads to premature failure of the new surface. A responsible contractor will be upfront about what they find during demolition and explain why each repair matters.

Tile, Coping, and Additional Features

Pool resurfacing often goes hand-in-hand with updating the waterline tile, coping (the cap around the pool's edge), and other decorative features. If your existing tile is cracked, outdated, or falling off, replacing it during resurfacing is the most practical and cost-effective time to do it.

The type of tile you choose — ceramic, porcelain, glass mosaic, or natural stone — will influence cost, as will the lineal footage around your pool. Similarly, replacing or repairing coping adds to the project scope. Many homeowners in Rancho Mirage and Palm Desert take the opportunity to upgrade to a more modern look, coordinating the new tile and coping with fresh paver decking around the pool for a complete transformation.

If your pool includes water features like spillovers, scuppers, or built-in fountains, those surfaces may also need refinishing. Spas attached to the main pool are essentially a second vessel that needs its own resurfacing, adding to both material and labor requirements.

Desert Climate and Water Chemistry Considerations

Southern California's desert climate plays a unique role in pool resurfacing that homeowners from other regions might not think about. In areas like Twentynine Palms, Cathedral City, and throughout the Coachella Valley, summer temperatures routinely exceed what most pool finish manufacturers consider ideal application conditions. This can affect scheduling — the best crews know when conditions are right for a high-quality application and will plan accordingly.

Water chemistry is another critical factor. The hard, mineral-rich water common across the Inland Empire and Coachella Valley can be aggressive on pool surfaces, especially standard plaster. Calcium buildup, scaling, and etching can shorten the life of a less durable finish. Homeowners who invest in a more resilient surface material often find it pays off in longevity and reduced maintenance.

A good contractor will also discuss proper startup chemistry after resurfacing. The first few weeks of water balancing are crucial to the long-term performance and warranty of your new pool finish.

Permits, Drainage, and Site Access

Depending on your city and the scope of the project, permits may be required for pool resurfacing — especially if structural modifications, electrical work, or plumbing changes are involved. Permit fees and the time needed to secure them can add to the overall project timeline and cost. Omdan Development Inc. (CSLB License #1148568) handles the permitting process so homeowners don't have to navigate it alone.

Site access is another factor that's easy to overlook. If your backyard is difficult to reach — narrow side yards, limited gate openings, or no direct access for equipment — the crew may need to hand-carry materials or use specialized equipment, which adds labor time. Proper drainage during the resurfacing process is also important; the old surface material and water need to go somewhere, and responsible disposal adds to the project logistics.

These practical considerations are part of why we always recommend a thorough on-site evaluation before quoting any pool remodeling project. What looks simple in photos can involve real-world challenges that only become clear in person.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my pool needs resurfacing or just cosmetic cleaning?

If you're seeing rough spots that snag swimsuits, visible etching, widespread staining that chemical treatments can't remove, or plaster flaking off in your hands, it's likely time to resurface rather than simply clean. A professional inspection can determine whether your pool's issues are surface-level or structural. Schedule a free on-site estimate with Omdan Development Inc. for an honest assessment.

How long does pool resurfacing take from start to finish?

Most residential pool resurfacing projects take roughly one to two weeks, depending on the scope of work, the surface material chosen, and whether additional repairs like tile or coping replacement are needed. Weather and proper curing time in the desert heat can also affect the timeline. Contact us for a free on-site estimate, and we'll give you a realistic project schedule for your specific pool.

What pool finish material lasts the longest in the desert climate?

Pebble-based finishes tend to offer the longest lifespan in desert environments because they resist the effects of UV exposure, hard water, and chemical wear better than standard plaster. Quartz finishes are also a strong mid-range option. The best choice depends on your priorities and budget, so we recommend scheduling a free on-site estimate to discuss the options for your home.

Can I resurface my pool and update the deck at the same time?

Yes, and it's often the smartest approach. Coordinating pool resurfacing with a new paver or concrete deck means less overall disruption and ensures a cohesive look.
