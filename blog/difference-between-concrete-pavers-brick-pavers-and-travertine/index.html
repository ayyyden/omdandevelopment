The Difference Between Concrete Pavers, Brick Pavers, and Travertine

Choosing the right paver material can make or break your outdoor space. Here's what every Coachella Valley and Inland Empire homeowner should know before breaking ground.

By Omdan Development Inc — Licensed Contractor, License #1148568

Why the Paver Material You Choose Matters

When homeowners in Palm Springs, Palm Desert, or anywhere across the Coachella Valley start planning a new patio, driveway, or pool deck, one of the first decisions they face is which paver material to use. It's not just about looks — though aesthetics obviously matter. The material you select affects how your outdoor surface handles the desert heat, how much maintenance it demands, and how long it lasts under the intense Southern California sun.

We've installed all three major paver types — concrete, brick, and travertine — across the Inland Empire and desert communities. Each one has legitimate strengths and trade-offs. There's no single "best" paver; there's only the best paver for your project, your climate, and your lifestyle. This guide will help you understand the real differences so you can make a confident decision.

Concrete Pavers: Versatile and Budget-Friendly

Concrete pavers are the most commonly installed paver type we see across Moreno Valley, Menifee, San Bernardino, and the broader Inland Empire. They're manufactured by pouring a mixture of cement, aggregate, and pigment into molds, which means they can be produced in a huge variety of shapes, colors, and textures. Want something that mimics natural stone? There's a concrete paver for that. Prefer a clean, modern geometric look? Concrete can do that too.

One of the biggest advantages of concrete pavers is their versatility. They work equally well on driveways, patios, walkways, and pool surrounds. They're strong enough to handle vehicle traffic when installed on a properly prepared base, and they're generally the most affordable of the three options discussed here. For homeowners who want a polished look without stretching their budget, concrete pavers are a reliable choice.

The main consideration with concrete pavers in the desert is color fading. Over years of direct UV exposure — and we get plenty of that in Yucca Valley and Twentynine Palms — some concrete pavers can lighten or lose vibrancy. Choosing higher-quality pavers with integral color (pigment mixed throughout, not just on the surface) significantly reduces this issue. Sealing your concrete pavers also helps preserve their appearance and makes them easier to clean.

Brick Pavers: Classic Appeal with Proven Durability

Brick pavers are made from natural clay that's fired in a kiln at extremely high temperatures. This process gives brick its characteristic warm tones — reds, browns, oranges, and earthy blends — and also makes it exceptionally durable. Brick pavers have been used for centuries, and there's a reason: they hold up remarkably well over time.

One of the standout qualities of brick is its color permanence. Because the color comes from the natural clay itself rather than added pigment, brick pavers don't fade the way some concrete pavers can. In a climate like Palm Desert or Desert Hot Springs where surfaces bake under the sun for most of the year, that kind of color stability is a real advantage. Brick also develops a natural patina over time that many homeowners find appealing.

Brick pavers do come with a narrower range of shapes and colors compared to concrete. If you're looking for a very specific modern aesthetic or unusual dimensions, concrete might offer more flexibility. But for traditional, Spanish-style, or Mediterranean-inspired homes — which are plentiful throughout the Coachella Valley — brick pavers feel right at home. They're excellent for walkways, patios, and garden borders, and they pair beautifully with gravel and xeriscape landscaping common in our desert communities.

Travertine Pavers: Natural Elegance for Upscale Outdoor Living

Travertine is a natural stone — a type of limestone formed by mineral deposits from hot springs and caves. It's been used in architecture for thousands of years (the Colosseum in Rome is largely built from travertine), and today it's one of the most sought-after materials for high-end outdoor spaces in Southern California.

What sets travertine apart is its natural beauty. Each piece has unique veining, color variation, and texture. It comes in shades of ivory, cream, walnut, gold, and silver, lending a sophisticated look that's difficult to replicate with manufactured materials. Travertine is a popular choice around pools because it stays remarkably cool underfoot — a quality that homeowners in Palm Springs and Temecula particularly appreciate during the scorching summer months. If you're planning a pool remodel, travertine coping and decking can completely elevate the finished result.

Travertine is naturally porous, which means it does require sealing to prevent staining and moisture absorption. In the desert, where we deal more with dust and dry conditions than heavy rain, maintenance is relatively manageable, but it's still something you need to stay on top of. Travertine is also the most expensive of the three options, both for the material itself and for installation, since it requires more precise handling and cutting.

How Each Paver Handles the Desert Climate

Southern California's desert and inland valleys present unique challenges for outdoor surfaces. Summer temperatures regularly push well above 100°F in areas like Desert Hot Springs, Twentynine Palms, and Yucca Valley. UV exposure is relentless. And while rainfall is minimal, when it does come, it can arrive in sudden, heavy downpours. All of these factors affect how each paver type performs over time.

Concrete pavers handle heat well and are very resistant to cracking, but as mentioned, prolonged UV exposure can cause fading in lower-quality products. Brick pavers are naturally heat-resistant and colorfast, making them a strong performer in desert conditions. Travertine pavers excel at staying cool underfoot, which is a major comfort advantage around pools and patios where people walk barefoot. All three types are permeable enough (especially when installed with sand joints) to handle water drainage effectively.

Regardless of which material you choose, proper installation is what determines long-term performance. A well-compacted base, correct joint spacing, and appropriate edge restraints matter far more than most homeowners realize. This is one area where working with an experienced paver installation contractor makes a significant difference in how your investment holds up over the years.

Best Uses for Each Paver Type

Each paver material has settings where it truly shines. Here's a practical breakdown to help you match the material to your project:

  • Concrete pavers — Best for driveways, large patios, commercial walkways, and any project where you want a wide selection of colors and patterns. A great all-around choice for homeowners in Murrieta, Menifee, and San Bernardino looking for durability on a reasonable budget.
  • Brick pavers — Ideal for walkways, garden paths, courtyard patios, and accent borders. Especially well-suited for homes with traditional, Spanish Colonial, or craftsman-style architecture.
  • Travertine pavers — Perfect for pool decks, upscale patios, outdoor kitchens, and entryways where you want a premium natural stone appearance. A favorite in Palm Springs, Palm Desert, and Temecula resort-style backyards.

Many projects actually benefit from combining materials. For instance, we've installed concrete paver driveways that transition into travertine pool decks, or brick walkways bordered by decorative gravel. Mixing materials thoughtfully creates visual interest and lets you allocate your budget where it matters most.

Maintenance and Long-Term Care

All three paver types are relatively low-maintenance compared to poured concrete slabs, which can develop large cracks that are expensive to repair. If an individual paver cracks or stains badly, you can replace just that one unit without tearing up the entire surface. That's a practical advantage that saves homeowners money over the life of the installation.

For concrete pavers, periodic sweeping and occasional rinsing with a garden hose are usually sufficient. Applying a quality sealer every few years helps maintain color and prevents weed growth between joints. Brick pavers need similar care, though their natural color resistance means you're mostly sealing for weed and moss prevention. Travertine requires the most attention — sealing is essential, and acidic cleaners should be avoided since they can etch the stone's surface.

In the Coachella Valley, blowing sand and dust are constant companions. All paver types benefit from regular sweeping or leaf-blowing to keep joints clean and the surface looking fresh. If you're investing in a paver project, factoring in a simple maintenance routine will protect your investment and keep your outdoor space looking its best for decades.
