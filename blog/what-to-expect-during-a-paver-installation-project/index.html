What to Expect During a Paver Installation Project

A step-by-step walkthrough of the paver installation process so you know exactly what's happening in your yard from day one to the finished product.

By Omdan Development Inc — Licensed Contractor, License #1148568

Why Understanding the Process Matters

If you've decided to upgrade your driveway, patio, walkway, or pool deck with pavers, you're making a smart investment — especially here in Southern California, where outdoor living is a year-round lifestyle. But like any construction project, the process involves more than just laying down stone. Knowing what to expect from start to finish helps you plan around the work, ask the right questions, and feel confident that the job is being done correctly.

At Omdan Development, we handle paver installations across Palm Springs, Palm Desert, Desert Hot Springs, Menifee, Temecula, and surrounding communities. We've found that homeowners who understand the phases of the project tend to be the most satisfied with the outcome. This guide walks you through each stage so nothing catches you off guard.

Phase 1: The Initial Consultation and Site Assessment

Every quality paver project starts with a conversation. During the initial consultation, your contractor should visit your property to evaluate the existing conditions of the area being paved. This means looking at the soil type, the slope and drainage patterns, any existing hardscape or structures, and how the space is currently used. In desert communities like Yucca Valley and Twentynine Palms, expansive sandy soils require different base preparation than the clay-heavy soils you might encounter in Moreno Valley or San Bernardino.

This is also the time to discuss your vision. Are you looking for a clean, modern look for a front walkway? A rustic, tumbled-stone patio around your pool? A durable driveway that can handle daily vehicle traffic? Your contractor will help you choose paver styles, colors, and patterns that complement your home and hold up to the local climate. The intense UV exposure and extreme heat we experience in the Coachella Valley are real factors that influence material selection.

After the site visit, you should receive a detailed scope of work that outlines the project dimensions, the materials to be used, and a realistic timeline. A reputable contractor will never pressure you to commit on the spot.

Phase 2: Preparing the Site

Site preparation is arguably the most important phase of a paver installation, and it's the part most homeowners never see done correctly by cut-rate installers. This step involves excavating the existing surface to the proper depth, which varies depending on whether the area will support foot traffic, vehicle traffic, or something in between. For a typical patio, you're looking at several inches of excavation. Driveways require significantly more depth to support the weight of cars.

Once the area is excavated, the crew grades the subsoil to establish proper drainage. Standing water is the enemy of any paver system. Even in our arid desert climate, monsoon rains and irrigation runoff can cause damage if the slope isn't correct. If your property also needs drainage improvements, this is a good time to address them — and if your home has gutter issues directing water toward the paver area, it's worth solving that problem simultaneously.

After grading, a layer of compacted base material — typically crushed aggregate — is spread and compacted in lifts using a plate compactor. This creates a rock-solid foundation that prevents shifting and settling over time. Cutting corners on the base is the number-one reason paver jobs fail, so pay attention to how much time your contractor spends on this phase.

Phase 3: Laying the Pavers

With a properly compacted base in place, a thin layer of bedding sand is screeded across the surface to create a smooth, level setting bed. This sand layer is what allows the pavers to be placed precisely and adjusted to the correct height. The pavers are then laid according to the chosen pattern — herringbone, running bond, basketweave, or a custom design.

This is the phase where the project really starts to take visual shape. Each paver is placed by hand, and border or edge pieces are cut with a diamond-blade saw to fit tightly against walls, curves, and other boundaries. If your project involves curves around a pool deck or transitions into an artificial turf area, the cutting and fitting work becomes more detailed and time-intensive.

Throughout the laying process, the crew checks alignment and levels constantly. Even a small deviation early on can compound into a noticeable problem across a large area. Experienced installers work methodically and don't rush this step.

Phase 4: Edge Restraints, Joint Sand, and Compaction

Once all the pavers are in position, the crew installs edge restraints along the perimeter. These are rigid borders — usually made of aluminum or heavy-duty plastic — that are spiked into the base material to prevent the pavers from creeping outward over time. Without proper edge restraints, even the best-laid pavers will gradually shift and develop gaps.

Next comes joint sand. Polymeric sand is swept into the joints between each paver, filling the gaps completely. When activated with water, polymeric sand hardens and locks the pavers together as a unified surface. This also inhibits weed growth and prevents insects from nesting between the stones — a real benefit in areas like Palm Desert and Menifee where ants are relentless.

Finally, the entire surface is compacted one more time with a plate compactor fitted with a protective pad. This settles the pavers firmly into the bedding sand and drives the joint sand deeper into the seams. The result is a surface that feels solid underfoot and won't shift when you walk, drive, or place furniture on it.

Phase 5: Final Cleanup and Sealing

After compaction, the crew cleans the surface of any excess sand and debris. This is also when your contractor should do a final walkthrough with you to inspect the finished work. Look at the pattern alignment, check that edges are straight and tight, and confirm that water flows away from your home and any structures.

Sealing is an optional but highly recommended finishing step, especially in the desert. A quality paver sealer enhances the color of the stone, provides UV protection against our punishing sun, and adds a layer of defense against staining from oil, food, or organic matter. In communities like Temecula and Murrieta, where homeowners often use their patios for outdoor entertaining, a sealed surface is much easier to maintain and keep looking sharp.

Your contractor should advise you on how long to wait before using the new surface — typically at least 24 to 48 hours to allow the polymeric sand and any sealer to fully cure.

How Pavers Complement Other Outdoor Upgrades

A paver installation often serves as the foundation — both literally and figuratively — for a broader outdoor transformation. Many of our clients in the Coachella Valley and Inland Empire combine their paver project with other improvements to get the most value from the work. For example, pairing a new paver patio with a gravel or xeriscape landscape creates a cohesive, low-maintenance yard that's perfectly suited to desert living.

If you're planning a pool remodeling project, new pool deck pavers can dramatically change the look and feel of the entire backyard. And if your home's exterior could use a refresh to match the new hardscape, exterior painting is a natural complement that ties everything together visually. Thinking about your outdoor space holistically — rather than as a series of isolated projects — often leads to better design decisions and less disruption overall.
