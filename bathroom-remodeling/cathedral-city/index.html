Bathroom remodeling services in Cathedral City

We handle the full range of bathroom work for Cathedral City residential and rental properties:

Shower Remodels & Original Tile Replacement

Original 1970s–80s shower surrounds in Cathedral City condos are among the most common remodel drivers we see. We remove old tile, waterproof the substrate correctly, and install new tile in a clean, contemporary pattern. Larger-format tile reduces grout lines and looks cleaner long-term. For MCM homes in Cathedral Canyon, we work within the scale and proportion of the original bathroom design.

Tub Removal & Tub-to-Shower Conversions

Many Cathedral City condo and rental owners want to remove original cast-iron tubs and convert to a walk-in shower. We handle demo, drain relocation if needed, waterproofing, and full tile installation. A curbless or low-threshold entry adds a modern feel, is easier to clean, and appeals to a wider range of tenants and guests.

Vanity Replacement

Original 1970s–80s laminate vanity cabinets with worn countertops are a visual liability in rental listings and a functional problem in day-to-day use. We demo the old unit, repair any wall damage behind it, and install a new vanity with appropriate plumbing connections. Floating vanities with quartz tops and updated mirrors transform a dated bathroom efficiently. For Cathedral Canyon MCM homes, we choose vanity styles that complement rather than clash with the architecture.

Tile Work & Flooring

We install new floor tile, wall tile, and shower surrounds that hold up to moisture and repeated cleaning. For Cathedral City condos that have had multiple flooring layers over the decades, we prep the substrate to a clean, level base before any new installation. See our tile work service for more detail on what we offer.

HOA-Compliant Condo Remodels

Condo bathroom remodels in Cathedral City HOA buildings require planning before demo ever starts. We provide written scope for board approval, schedule within approved work hours, protect common areas and elevator access, and coordinate material delivery to minimize building impact. We photograph completion for HOA records. This is standard process for us — not an exception.

Waterproofing, Drywall & Finish Work

Behind every bathroom remodel is a properly waterproofed wet area and clean drywall finish. We use cement board and appropriate membranes in shower and tub surrounds, and handle all drywall repair needed as part of the remodel. Ventilation improvements are included when the existing exhaust is inadequate — common in older Cathedral City condo units. See our drywall repair service for standalone patching work.

Remodeling Cathedral City condos, MCM homes, and rental properties

Cathedral City's two dominant property types each require a different remodeling approach. The dense condo corridor along Date Palm Drive and surrounding streets has buildings where original 1970s–80s bathrooms are still in place — some with original tile, cast-iron tubs, and fixtures that have been in service for 40 to 50 years. These properties need contractors who understand HOA compliance, multi-unit access logistics, and the structural realities of remodeling in older building construction.

The growing Cathedral City rental market creates a separate incentive to upgrade. Condo owners on Date Palm Drive who update bathrooms from dated originals to clean, modern finishes consistently see improved rental rates and shorter vacancy periods. The upgrade pays for itself over a reasonable timeframe.

MCM and MCM-adjacent homes in Cathedral Canyon require care. These properties often have bathroom tile, fixtures, and hardware with period character that shouldn't be torn out indiscriminately. We assess what's worth keeping — or worth replicating — and present a plan that modernizes what needs it without erasing what makes the home distinctive.

Signs it may be time to remodel your Cathedral City bathroom

  • Original 1970s–80s tile colors — pink, avocado, harvest gold — are dating the unit and affecting rental or resale appeal
  • Cast-iron tub enamel is chipped, stained, or discolored beyond what refinishing can fix
  • Laminate vanity cabinet is swollen, delaminating, or the countertop surface is worn through
  • Grout in the shower has cracked or pulled away, allowing moisture behind the tile
  • Tenant feedback or rental listing reviews consistently identify the bathroom as the unit's weakest feature

Bathroom remodel FAQ — Cathedral City

Can you remodel bathrooms in Cathedral City condo buildings with HOA restrictions?

Yes. Condo bathroom remodels in HOA-managed buildings require board notification, work-hour compliance, and common-area protection. We provide written scope for HOA submissions before work begins, schedule within approved hours, and protect building common areas throughout.

What does a 1970s-80s Cathedral City condo bathroom typically need?

Original 1970s–80s condo bathrooms typically have pink, avocado, or harvest gold tile, cast-iron tubs with worn enamel, laminate vanity cabinets, and corroded fixtures. A full remodel addresses all of these. Targeted upgrades — just the vanity, or just the shower — are also available if budget or HOA timing calls for a phased approach.

Do you work on MCM bathrooms in Cathedral Canyon without altering the home's character?

Yes. MCM homes in Cathedral Canyon often have bathrooms with period tile, hardware, and proportions worth preserving. We approach these carefully — replacing what's worn while keeping details that complement the home's original character. We present options and get your approval before any installation affecting period elements.

Can upgrading a bathroom increase rental income in Cathedral City?

Yes. Cathedral City has a growing rental market. Owners on Date Palm Drive who upgrade from original 1970s tile to a modern shower, new vanity, and updated flooring consistently see improved rental rates and faster unit turnover. We can work around existing tenancy windows to minimize vacancy.

How do I get a bathroom remodeling estimate in Cathedral City?

Call or text (951) 292-0703 with photos of the existing bathroom and a description of what you want to change. For condo and HOA properties, include building access details and any known work-hour restrictions. We'll respond with next steps and a free estimate.
