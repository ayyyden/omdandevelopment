Window replacement in Moreno Valley — tract homes reaching upgrade age

Moreno Valley grew rapidly during the 1980s and 1990s, and a large portion of its housing stock was built during those decades. Neighborhoods like Sunnymead Ranch, Alessandro Heights, Lake Hills Estates, Edgemont, and areas near Lake Perris are filled with tract homes that were built with minimum-code builder-grade windows. After 25–40 years, those windows are at the natural end of their useful life — and Inland Empire summers make that degradation happen faster.

Moreno Valley summers regularly exceed 105°F, and without proper Low-E glass, heat radiates through east and west-facing windows continuously during the hottest months. Most builder-grade windows from this era have little or no Low-E coating — the technology existed but was not standard in lower-cost construction. Replacement delivers a measurable improvement in summer comfort and reduces the demand on air conditioning systems.

Moreno Valley window considerations

  • Standard sizes make retrofit efficient. Tract construction uses standardized rough opening sizes, which means retrofit replacement units are readily available and installation is faster and less disruptive than in custom or older homes with non-standard openings.
  • Aluminum frames age poorly in Inland Empire heat. Builder-grade aluminum frames from this era show significant weatherstripping failure, frame oxidation, and sliding mechanism wear. Replacement with vinyl frames eliminates all these failure modes permanently.
  • West-facing windows most impactful. Moreno Valley homes with west-facing living areas bear the full brunt of afternoon sun from June through September. Correctly specified Low-E glass for west exposures reduces radiant heat in these rooms substantially — often the most noticeable single improvement in the home.
  • Alessandro Heights and hillside homes. Properties in higher-elevation hillside neighborhoods like Alessandro Heights often have larger windows for views. These benefit from both the comfort and UV-protection aspects of Low-E glass — view glass is typically the largest source of UV-related interior fading.

Moreno Valley window FAQ

What's wrong with the original windows in 1990s Moreno Valley homes?

Builder-grade windows from this era typically used aluminum frames with minimal or no Low-E coating. Aluminum conducts heat directly into the home, and without a Low-E coating, glass transmits solar infrared freely. After 30+ years, weatherstripping is degraded, sliders are stiff, and the glass itself provides far less efficiency than modern options. Replacement is the right call at this age.

How many windows can be replaced in a typical tract home in one day?

A typical 3–4 bedroom Moreno Valley tract home with 10–15 windows can often be completed in one to two days. Standard opening sizes and retrofit replacement make this one of the more efficient installation types we do.

Do you serve Alessandro Heights and Lake Perris area homes?

Yes. We serve all neighborhoods in Moreno Valley. Hillside properties in Alessandro Heights and larger homes near Lake Perris often have view glass and larger openings that benefit the most from Low-E upgrades.

Are permits required for window replacement in Moreno Valley?

For like-for-like replacements of the same size and type, permits are typically not required by the City of Moreno Valley. We identify whether permits are needed for your specific project and pull them when required.

Are you licensed for window work in Moreno Valley?

Yes. Omdan Development Inc holds CSLB License #1148568 and is fully licensed, bonded, and insured for window installation in Moreno Valley and throughout the Inland Empire.
