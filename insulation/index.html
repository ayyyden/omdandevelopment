Insulation Services We Offer

Every approach has a right fit — we recommend based on your home's construction.

Attic Insulation

The top priority for desert homes. Blown-in or batt at R-38 to R-49 for attic floors and rafters to block summer heat before it reaches living space.

Spray Foam Insulation

Closed-cell spray foam for cathedral ceilings, rim joists, and tight areas where blown-in won't reach. Provides both insulation and an air barrier in one step.

Blown-In Fiberglass & Cellulose

Fast, even coverage for large attic floors. Cost-effective for homes with open attic access and no major air sealing issues.

Wall Insulation

Dense-pack blown-in for exterior walls without opening the drywall. Drilled from exterior or interior depending on home construction.

Air Sealing

Sealing gaps around penetrations, attic hatches, recessed lights, and HVAC boots before insulating — because insulation without air sealing only does half the job.

Crawl Space & Under-Floor

Vapor barriers and under-floor insulation for homes with crawl spaces. Keeps moisture out and conditioned air in.

Why Insulation Has the Fastest Payback of Any Home Upgrade

Energy experts consistently rank attic insulation as the highest return home energy improvement available — especially in hot climates. The savings begin the day the job is complete and continue every month, every year, for the life of the home.

  • →Immediate reduction in cooling runtime. A well-insulated attic blocks the primary pathway of summer heat into your home. Your A/C reaches setpoint faster and holds it longer between cycles.
  • →HVAC system lasts longer. When your unit runs less, it accumulates fewer operating hours. Compressors, capacitors, and coils all last significantly longer with reduced runtime — deferring expensive equipment replacement.
  • →Air sealing compounds the savings. Insulation and air sealing together can reduce heating and cooling energy use by 20–40% in desert climates. The combination works far better than either alone.
  • →Rebate programs available. Southern California Edison and Imperial Irrigation District both offer insulation rebates for qualifying upgrades. We can help you navigate what applies to your home and utility.
  • →Quieter home. Insulation absorbs sound, reducing noise transfer between floors, through ceilings, and from outside — a benefit that is easy to underestimate until you experience it.

Understanding R-value in the desert

What is R-value?

R-value measures resistance to heat flow. Higher R = better insulation. Doubling R-value does not halve your energy use, but every R increment still matters — especially in extreme heat.

What does Palm Springs need?

DOE recommends R-38 to R-49 for attics in Climate Zone 15. Many older valley homes have R-11 or less — a massive gap that directly drives high summer utility bills.

Why air sealing comes first

Insulation slows conductive heat transfer. Air sealing stops convective transfer — air physically moving through gaps. Without air sealing, even high-R insulation underperforms because hot air bypasses it entirely.

Spray foam vs. blown-in

Spray foam provides both insulation and air sealing in one product. Blown-in is faster and less expensive for open attic floors but does not air-seal on its own — both have the right applications depending on your home's construction.

Common Questions About Insulation

What homeowners ask us most — answered honestly.

How do I know if I have enough insulation right now?

Go into your attic (early morning before it heats up) and look at the attic floor. If you can see the tops of the floor joists, your insulation is below DOE recommendations — even a little bit of visible joist means you're underinsulated by desert standards. If your A/C runs nearly nonstop on hot days despite a reasonable thermostat setting, insufficient attic insulation is almost always a contributing factor. We can assess during a quick walk-through.

Is spray foam worth it compared to blown-in fiberglass?

For open attic floors with good access, blown-in fiberglass is often the right choice — it covers large areas quickly and economically. Spray foam earns its premium in specific situations: cathedral ceilings where you cannot access the space above, tight cavities where blown-in cannot reach, around penetrations (pipes, wires, ducts) where air sealing is critical, and rim joists. The best approach often uses both — blown-in for the field and spray foam at the perimeter and penetrations.

Will adding insulation fix my hot rooms?

Often yes, but not always alone. Hot rooms in summer are typically caused by a combination of: inadequate attic insulation above the room, solar heat gain through nearby windows, leaky ducts delivering less conditioned air than the system intends, or an HVAC system that is undersized for the home's layout. We diagnose the actual cause before recommending a solution — in some homes the fix is insulation, in others it is duct sealing or HVAC adjustment.

Does insulation work the same in a flat-roof home?

Flat-roof homes (common in Palm Springs design) present unique challenges. There is no attic space between the roof membrane and the ceiling — so the insulation layer is typically either on top of the roof deck or inside the ceiling cavity. Many mid-century flat-roof homes have very little insulation in this assembly. Adding tapered rigid foam insulation above the roof deck during a re-roof is one of the most effective upgrades possible for this home type.

Do I need to remove old insulation before adding new?

Not always. If existing insulation is clean, dry, and pest-free, we can add new material on top — especially for blown-in attic applications. Removal is necessary when there is rodent or pest contamination (the smell never goes away through new insulation), moisture damage or mold, suspected asbestos-containing vermiculite (pre-1980 homes), or existing material that has settled below effective R-value. We assess during the initial inspection and tell you clearly what is needed and why.

What is a blower door test and do I need one?

A blower door test depressurizes the house and measures how much air is moving through the building envelope — quantifying air leakage in real terms. It is the most accurate way to identify where sealing is needed before insulation is installed. Some utility rebate programs require a blower door test to qualify. It is not required for every project, but for homes with high utility bills that don't seem to respond to other improvements, it often reveals the hidden cause.

What affects the cost of attic or spray foam insulation?

The cost depends on your home: the attic or wall square footage, the type of insulation (blown-in or spray foam), whether old insulation has to be removed, air sealing, and attic access. Because no two homes are the same, we don't publish one-size-fits-all prices. We'll visit your home, measure, and give you personalized pricing with a free on-site estimate.
